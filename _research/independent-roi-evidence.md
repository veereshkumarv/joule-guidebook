# Independent evidence on enterprise GenAI and agentic AI ROI, including the skeptical evidence

Research date: 2026-11-23. Prepared for the SAP-client AI value guidebook.

**Tooling note:** No WebSearch tool was available. Sources were opened with WebFetch, direct HTTP fetches, and Internet Archive snapshots where the live site returned 403 (Gartner, McKinsey, BCG, PwC). Discovery used the Hacker News Algolia API and Bing HTML. Every figure below was read from the page or PDF cited. Where a page came from an archive snapshot, the original URL is given and the snapshot date is noted.

**Type labels:** ACADEMIC = peer-reviewed or academic working paper; ANALYST = Gartner, Forrester, IDC, S&P; SURVEY = consultancy or vendor survey (sample size and sponsor noted); VENDOR-CLAIMED = a company's own claims about its own deployment; NEWS = reported case.

**Confidence scale:** High = primary source opened and figure read verbatim. Medium = figure read from a secondary report of the primary source, or the methodology is weak. Low = single anecdote, or the method is unclear.

## 2. What the skeptics are right about

1. **Most enterprises cannot yet show enterprise-level P&L impact.**
   - McKinsey 2026: only 37% attribute any EBIT impact to AI, unchanged from the year before, and only about 6% are high performers.
   - PwC 2026: 56% of 4,454 CEOs see "no significant financial benefit to date".
   - BCG 2025: 60% get "no material value".
   - Forrester 2025: 15% saw an EBITDA lift.
   - IBM 2025: CEOs say 25% of initiatives delivered the expected ROI.

   These surveys use different samples and methods but agree on direction.
2. **Pilot-to-production attrition is severe.**
   - S&P 2025: 42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives (up from 17%), and 46% of POCs on average are scrapped.
   - IDC/Lenovo: 4 of 33 POCs reach production.
   - Gartner forecasts at least 30% GenAI POC abandonment and over 40% agentic project cancellations by 2027.
3. **Individual productivity is not the same as firm productivity.**
   - Workers report time savings: St. Louis Fed 5.4% of hours for users; BCG 2026, 42% of frontline regular users save 8 hours a week.
   - But BCG finds 66% get no guidance on what to do with the time saved.
   - Danish administrative data show null effects on earnings and hours, ruling out effects above 2%.
   - Nearly 9,000 executives report only a 0.2% gain in annual labor productivity growth.
   - Coding studies show large output gains that shrink to 30% more releases and no extra app usage.
4. **Costs are rising and poorly tracked.**
   - Gartner predicts inference cost per agentic workflow will rise more than fivefold through 2028.
   - Deloitte 2026: only 44% track per-unit inference cost.
   - McKinsey 2026: about 20% say operating and token costs constrain use.
5. **Self-perception is unreliable.** In the METR RCT, developers believed they were 20% faster but were 19% slower.
6. **"Replace the humans" deployments get reversed.** Klarna rehired humans after quality dropped, CBA reversed its voice-bot redundancies, McDonald's ended its IBM drive-thru test, and Deloitte refunded fees over a hallucinated report.
7. **Vendor hype is real.** Gartner: only about 130 of "thousands" of agentic vendors are real ("agent washing").

**But the skeptic headline is weaker than it looks.** The MIT "95%" figure comes from 52 interviews, 153 conference surveys, and a narrow six-month, deployment-with-KPIs definition. Fortune's description of the sample does not match the PDF. Most "ROI" survey numbers, positive or negative, are self-reported by executives, and most come from firms that sell AI services: McKinsey, BCG, Deloitte, PwC, IBM, and IDC with Lenovo.

## 3. What distinguishes projects that do return value

- **Buy or partner rather than build from scratch.** MIT NANDA found about 67% deployment success for external partnerships against about 33% for internal builds. The finding comes from interviews and the report says it is correlational. It is relevant for SAP clients choosing embedded or vendor AI over custom builds.
  - Counter-signal: McKinsey 2026 reports that 32% have chosen to build some software in-house with coding agents instead of buying.
- **Back-office, external-spend targets.** MIT NANDA's documented savings came from replacing BPO and agency spend ($2–10M a year BPO elimination; 30% agency cost cut), not from cutting internal headcount. McKinsey 2026 reports cost reductions most often in supply chain, service operations, and manufacturing. AI Index and McKinsey 2025 data link the highest cost savings to software engineering and manufacturing (56%).
- **Narrow, structured, measurable tasks.** The AI Index 2026 says gains are "largest in structured, measurable work". Examples:
  - 14% gain in customer support, 34% for novices (Brynjolfsson).
  - Inside-the-frontier consulting tasks gain while outside-the-frontier tasks get 19 points worse (Dell'Acqua).
- **Workflow redesign, not bolt-on.**
  - McKinsey high performers "fundamentally redesign workflows".
  - BCG's 10-20-70 rule: 70% of the effort is people and process.
  - Wharton 2026: departments that let agents take consequential actions, with automated monitoring, transformed more.
  - Gartner advises "rethinking workflows with agentic AI from the ground up".
- **Measure the right outcome.**
  - Wharton 2026: 35% strongly positive ROI when value is measured in growth terms against 13% for efficiency-only.
  - PwC: firms with strong foundations are three times more likely to report returns.
  - BCG: 60% of future-built firms rigorously track AI value.
- **Data readiness and the right problem.** RAND's root causes are wrong problem, insufficient data, technology-first thinking, infrastructure, and over-hard problems. Gartner's reasons for POC abandonment are poor data quality, risk controls, cost, and unclear value. IBM: 50% of CEOs say rapid investment left "disconnected, piecemeal technology".
- **Leadership support and accountable ownership.** BCG: positive sentiment rises from 15% to 55% with strong leadership support. MIT: decentralized implementation authority with clear accountability. IBM 2026: AI-first C-suite design scaled 10% more initiatives.
- **Cost engineering for agents.** Gartner: route routine steps to cheaper or open models (about 90% of steps), and use frontier reasoning only where needed. Deloitte: "trace cost to the token".
- **Keep humans in the loop for customer-facing quality.** Klarna and CBA show that full replacement of customer contact can backfire.

## 4. Evidence on what builds stakeholder trust and confidence in AI deployments

**Headline:** There is good evidence on where trust is low and why. There is **little controlled evidence on which interventions raise trust or adoption in enterprise deployments**. Most "what works" claims below are self-reported correlations, stated intentions, or small lab experiments. The guidebook should present them as design hypotheses, not proven levers.

## 5. Gaps and unverified items

- IDC/Forrester Total Economic Impact (TEI) ROI studies are vendor-commissioned by design (for example, Microsoft- or SAP-commissioned TEIs). None were opened in this pass, so treat any TEI ROI figure as VENDOR-CLAIMED and UNVERIFIED here.
- The Bloomberg original of the Klarna interview (paywalled) was not opened; it is cited via Fortune and CNBC.
- The Gartner 2026 survey "21% of service and support leaders have reduced human agent headcount because of AI" was seen only as a headline on the Gartner newsroom; the body was not read. UNVERIFIED detail.
- Sample sizes for the IBM CAIO study (Nov 2026) and the Wharton 2026 report were not captured.
- No Gartner update was found confirming whether the "30% abandoned by end-2025" prediction materialized. The S&P 2025 data (42% abandon most initiatives) is the nearest measured figure.
- The MIT NANDA report is hosted on a third-party domain (mlq.ai). No MIT-hosted copy was confirmed, and MIT has not published a peer-reviewed version.

**Gaps specific to section 4 (trust):**

- No controlled or quasi-experimental study was found showing that any enterprise trust intervention (explanations, citations, staged rollout, governance programs, training) raises adoption or ROI. Items in 4e rest on surveys, stated intent, and small lab tasks.
- Gartner's own press release for the Oct 2025 agent-trust survey was not opened; the 15%, 19%, and 74% figures come from The Register's report of it (UNVERIFIED against the primary).
- The Deloitte trust-specific studies, any Forrester trust or AI-governance survey, and the Edelman Trust Barometer main report (as opposed to the Oct 2025 flash poll) were not opened.
- The AI Index risk "considered relevant vs. actively mitigated" chart could not be reliably extracted from the PDF text, so no figures from it are quoted.
- The KPMG/Melbourne "AI at work" 66% and 56% figures do not state their respondent base on the page read; the full report PDF was not opened.
- ISO/IEC 42001 scope text and any certification-uptake statistics were not read.
- The EU AI Act high-risk dates were read from a secondary tracking site (artificialintelligenceact.eu), not the Official Journal. The apparent postponement to 2 December 2027 needs confirmation.
- Outcome-based and risk-sharing pricing: only a vendor price page (Intercom) was opened. An OpenAI post on "outcome-based pricing" (Jan 2026) and a critical Lago blog post were seen only as Hacker News titles and are UNVERIFIED. No independent evidence on effects was found.
- The legal-research hallucination study covers 2024 product versions in a legal setting; no equivalent independent test for SAP, ERP, finance, or procurement copilots was found.

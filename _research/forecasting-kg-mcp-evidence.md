## 3. Trust and accuracy evidence (summary for client confidence)

### 3.1 What can honestly be said about knowledge-graph / semantic-layer grounding

- Best KG-for-enterprise-data number found: 16% to 54% GPT-4 zero-shot accuracy, SQL vs knowledge-graph layer, insurance schema, KG-vendor authors, preprint, abstract only (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07509). ## 4. What this means for design

1. Do not sell the knowledge graph as an accuracy multiplier. The evidence supports it for multi-hop relationship questions (supplier to part to plant to order) and for text-to-query over a governed semantic model, and not for simple lookups. Use the graph where the question needs joins across entities (shortage root cause, multi-tier exposure), and plain governed queries or IBP key figures for single-number lookups. Pilot on the client's own questions and report accuracy and abstention separately (the 72% result included 8% "I don't know").
2. Keep forecasting in the planning engine, and use the LLM and graph for explanation and exception handling. Evidence favours tuned gradient boosting and ensembles on intermittent retail data (M5), combinations over single models (M4, per a secondary source), and says foundation models are competitive zero-shot but show leakage caveats (GIFT-Eval) and weaknesses on trends and short context (Chronos paper). IBP already ships gradient-boosting demand sensing at daily periodicity. SAP-RPT-1 is a classification and regression model on tables with no fine-tuning: treat it as a candidate for features such as supplier delay risk, not as a replacement for the time-series forecaster, and benchmark it against a simple baseline.
3. Make Forecast Value Added the acceptance test. Measure every model, every agent proposal and every planner override against a naive and a statistical baseline at the planner's level and lag. The SLB case (50% to about 90% on some lines) shows the size of claim vendors make; it has no counterfactual, so do not reuse it as a promise.
4. Design for verification cost, not for the appearance of trust. Explanations and citations raised acceptance whether or not the AI was right (Bansal et al.), and only reduced overreliance when verification was cheap (Vasconcelos et al.). Each shortage alert should show the underlying order, stock and supplier record with one click, a recomputable quantity, and the graph path as a short list, and sample-audit citation correctness (the search-engine audit found 74.5% citation precision).
5. Treat MCP as an untrusted integration boundary. Use the MCP authorization profile in spec 2026-07-28 (resource indicators, audience validation, no token passthrough), per-user principal propagation (as shown in the Integration Suite MCP Gateway material), least-privilege scopes, read-only tools for forecasting and shortage questions, human approval for any write such as purchase requisitions, pinned and reviewed tool descriptions to counter tool poisoning, and a registry of approved servers. Dev-time servers (cap-js/mcp-server, MDK, ADT) should stay out of production data paths.
6. Use SAP trust artefacts with attribution. Say "SAP states ISO/IEC 42001 certification covering Joule, AI Core and AI Launchpad" and request the certificate; confirm that content filtering, masking and grounding are in the licensed AI Core plan; confirm GA state of each Joule assistant (several are "planned for Q4 2026" or in phased GA as of 2026-10).
7. Guard against alert fatigue and churn. The Exception Management Agent investigates alerts and proposes mitigations, which suggests volume is a known problem, but no opened source quantified alert fatigue or bullwhip effects. Add alert precision and planner action rate to the pilot metrics, and monitor forecast stability between runs (the demand-planning stability paper found ensembles help).

# Comparable cases and benchmarks for SAP AI use-case families

Research date: 2026-11-23. STATUS: complete for what could be opened; coverage gaps are listed in each family and at the end.

## Method and tool note (read first)

- **No dedicated web-search tool was available.** Pages were opened with WebFetch and with curl plus a text extractor. Search engines mostly returned bot challenges or rate limits (Brave intermittently, DuckDuckGo/Google/Bing blocked or irrelevant), so discovery was constrained. Some families have thin coverage because of this, not because evidence doesn't exist.
- Every row below was **opened and read** at the URL shown unless marked UNVERIFIED.
- Type labels: VENDOR-CLAIMED, CUSTOMER-REPORTED, INDEPENDENT-ACADEMIC, GOVERNMENT-EVALUATION, INDUSTRY-SURVEY (independent research or analyst survey, self-reported).
- Confidence: High = controlled or large-sample design with method disclosed; Medium = large self-report or quasi-experimental; Low = vendor or marketing claim without a method.

### How to use these in a conservative business case

1. Start from measured or government-evaluated numbers (HMRC ~12 min/day, METR/Google for developers) and only use vendor claims as upside scenarios.
2. Convert time saved to capacity, not headcount savings. DBT's null productivity finding is the key warning.
3. Apply a utilisation haircut (22% to 83% active) to every per-licence benefit.
4. For SAP-specific items (ABAP, ATC/clean-core remediation, SAP test automation, Joule agents) no independent evidence was found; require an internal controlled pilot before committing a benefit.
5. Budget separately for training and change management, which every government trial calls essential even though no verified cost share was found.
